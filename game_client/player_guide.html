Player Guide

Everything a new commander needs to know before the first turn.

1How to Win

The goal

Move any one of your robots into the hex where the enemy Captain is standing. That is a Capture, and your team wins on the spot.

Each team has exactly one Captain. You win by capturing theirs, and you lose if they capture yours. So every turn you are doing two jobs at once: getting a robot to the enemy Captain, and keeping enemy robots away from your own.

  • Any robot can make the capture. Strength does not matter here. Even an Engineer, the weakest robot, wins the game if it steps onto the enemy Captain.
  • The capture has to be a move. Your robot must step into the Captain's hex during a round, or, for a Scout, leap into it. Standing next to it is not enough.
  • Watch for enemy Scouts. A Scout can leap onto your Captain from up to 3 hexes away, straight over water, forest and other robots.
  • The game ends the moment it happens. Nothing else in that turn is played out.

How a turn works

  1. Plan. Everyone plans at the same time. You draw a path for each of your robots on the map. A turn has 6 rounds, and in each round a robot either moves one hex or waits. Once per turn, a Scout can instead leap 2 or 3 hexes. You cannot see what anyone else is planning.
  2. Submit. Press Submit Turn (or Enter) before the timer runs out. If time runs out, your plan is sent as it stands. Any round you left empty becomes a wait.
  3. Watch the replay. When every player has submitted, the server plays all the moves together and everyone watches the same replay.

Who goes first

Inside each round, robots act one at a time. The order matters when two robots try for the same hex.

  • The team with the Initiative moves all of its robots first. Initiative swaps between the teams every turn, and the top bar shows who has it.
  • Within a team, Engineers act first, then the Captain, then Scouts and Defenders.
Tip

Your plan can go wrong. If a move is blocked by a teammate or by an enemy at least as strong, your robot stays put and tries that same move again next round. Every later step slides one round later, and steps pushed past round 6 don't happen. Leave a spare round or two when timing matters.

2The Robots

There are four kinds of robot. They differ in how far they can move each turn, how strong they are, how far they can see, and which special ability they have.

Rules every robot follows

  • Movement. A move goes to one of the six neighbouring hexes. The one exception is a Scout's leap, which covers 2 or 3 hexes in a single move. Each robot has a limited number of moves per turn, and the rounds it does not move in are waits. You can mix moves and waits in any order.
  • Blocked hexes. Robots cannot enter water or forest, and they cannot move onto a teammate. A leaping Scout can pass over water and forest, but it can't land on them.
  • Strength. When a robot tries to move onto an enemy, the stronger one wins. See section 3.
  • Energy and abilities. Using a special ability (pressing Deploy) costs 1 energy. A robot with no energy cannot use its ability. Each robot type has a maximum energy it can hold.
  • Sight. Each robot sees enemies within its sight range.

Captain

The robot you must protect. Lose it and you lose the game.
3Moves
7Strength
2Max energy
5Sight

Movement: up to 3 moves per turn. The Captain is the strongest robot in the game, so it can shove any other enemy robot out of its way. However, any enemy robot that steps onto it, however weak, wins the game.

Special ability: EMP

Fires an electromagnetic pulse at a hex 4 to 6 hexes away in a straight line. The pulse covers the target and every hex within 3 of it.

  • Any robot that waits on an EMP hex is stunned where it stands for the rest of the turn. Robots that only pass through are not affected.
  • The EMP stays on the map and does not wear off.
  • It affects your own robots too, so plan your waits carefully.

Scout

The fast hunter. It sees far and covers ground quickly.
4Moves
3Strength
6Max energy
7Sight

Movement: up to 4 moves per turn, and one of them can be a leap. Scouts have the longest sight range, which makes them your eyes on the battlefield. They are fairly weak: they can push an Engineer aside, but Defenders and Captains will knock them back.

Special ability: Leap

Jumps 2 or 3 hexes in a single move, straight over anything in between: water, forest, firewalls and other robots. It's the only safe way past a firewall, but landing on a firewall hex still triggers it.

  • Press Deploy (or F), then pick one of the purple hexes. They are every open hex 2 or 3 away. The Scout can't land on water or forest.
  • A leap costs 1 energy and counts as one of the Scout's 4 moves. A Scout can leap once per turn.
  • Landing works like stepping in: land on a weaker enemy to knock it home, or land on the enemy Captain to win.
  • If the landing hex is blocked, the Scout stays put and tries the same leap next round. The energy is only spent once the leap actually happens.

Defender

The slow, sturdy guard that keeps hunters away from your Captain.
2Moves
5Strength
3Max energy
3Sight

Movement: only 2 moves per turn, and it has the shortest sight range. What it has is strength. A Defender beats Scouts and Engineers, so a Defender next to your Captain stops enemy Scouts from stepping in. Enemy Scouts can leap over a Defender, though, so a single Defender can't seal off your Captain. Cover the hexes 2 or 3 away as well.

Special ability: Firewall

Lays a firewall in a straight line of 4 hexes running out from the Defender.

  • Any robot that steps onto a firewall hex, or waits on one, is sent back to its starting hex and stunned. That piece of firewall burns out after it catches someone; the rest of the line stays.
  • Firewalls are permanent. A piece stays on the map until it catches a robot.
  • It affects your own robots too. A leaping Scout can jump over it safely, but landing on it counts.
  • A new firewall becomes active from the round after it's placed.

Engineer

The quick support robot. It keeps the team supplied with energy.
6Moves

3Max energy

Movement: can move in all 6 rounds, more than any other robot, and it always acts first in its team's order. With zero strength, every enemy robot beats it and it can never push anyone aside. Like any robot, though, it wins the game if it reaches the enemy Captain.

Special ability: Supply Drop

Places a supply crate on a neighbouring hex. It cannot go on a blocked hex or on a hex that already has a crate.

  • The first robot to move onto the crate picks it up and gains 1 energy, up to its maximum. The crate is then gone.
  • Anyone can grab it, enemies included, so drop crates where your team will reach them first.

3Getting Knocked Out

Robots are never removed from the game. A robot that loses a fight or walks into a trap is knocked out instead. Sometimes it is also sent back to where it started.

What "stunned" means
  • A stunned robot does nothing for the rest of the turn. Its remaining planned steps are skipped.
  • It recovers automatically at the start of the next turn, with its energy unchanged.
  • While stunned, it does not block anything. Other robots can move straight through its hex, and a stunned Captain cannot be captured.

There are four ways to be knocked out:

1. Pushed by a stronger enemy

Sent home + stunned

If an enemy with higher strength moves onto your robot's hex, your robot is shoved all the way back to its starting hex and stunned. The attacker takes the hex. Leaping onto a robot counts too, so an enemy Scout can knock home an Engineer from 3 hexes away.

Strength order: Captain 7 > Defender 5 > Scout 3 > Engineer 0.

2. Sharing a hex with a stronger enemy

Sent home + stunned

Because stunned robots don't block, a robot can end up in the same hex as an enemy. When the weaker one next acts, it is sent back to its starting hex and stunned.

3. Entering a Firewall

Sent home + stunned

A robot that steps onto a firewall hex, or waits on one, is sent back to its starting hex and stunned. That piece of firewall is used up. Only a Scout leaping over it gets past safely. When you plan a path onto a known firewall, the robot card warns you.

4. Waiting in an EMP

Stunned in place

A robot that waits on an EMP hex is stunned where it stands. The EMP stays on the map, so it is dangerous to wait there on later turns too. Moving through it is safe.

What does not knock you out

  • Bumping into an equal or stronger enemy. Your robot stays where it is and tries the same move again next round. Robots of equal strength block each other.
  • Bumping into a teammate. Same result: you stay put and try again next round, so if the teammate moves away, you follow.
Why getting sent home hurts

Every robot's starting hex is at your team's spawn on the edge of the map. A Scout knocked home from the middle of the map can lose two or three turns walking back. The opposite is also true: knocking enemy robots home is the best way to clear a path to their Captain.
